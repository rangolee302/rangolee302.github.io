A producer should not have to guess what was built

Most portfolios stop at a title and a genre. These pages keep the input, the engine, and the part that was hard, next to the picture of the build.

  • Speech without a network

    Speaking Dead scores a spoken word with an on device library, so play does not wait on a connection.

  • A puzzle that reached a store

    Flippyland is on Google Play. After playtests, free camera swipes were replaced with buttons because the first control was hard to read.

  • Movement as the lesson

    Luca & Friends uses MediaPipe pose landmarks inside an AR lesson. Firebase keeps accounts and progress for a parent.

  • Shared play, written down

    VR Monopoly takes Kinect motion into a Unity board and syncs a LAN match. Survival Camping is a co op prototype still in prototype stage.

Questions studios ask first

Who is this page for?

Studios and producers who want to see Unity work before they write. It is not a store page for players.

What should the first email say?

Platform, the input you care about, and whether you need a prototype or something closer to a store build.

Which projects are on a store?

Luca & Friends links to the App Store and Google Play from its writeup. Flippyland links to Google Play from its writeup.

Are the others finished products?

No. Speaking Dead and VR Monopoly are documented builds. Survival Camping is still a prototype.

Is every project solo?

No. The VR Monopoly and Luca pages describe team work. Flippyland records a control decision in the first person.

Can I see work that is not listed?

Yes. The older site asked people to email rangolee302@gmail.com for projects that are not public here.
